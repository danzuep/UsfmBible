A simple, free and open source Bible reader for Android with a minimalist design. Choose from 1,500+ translations in hundreds of languages, download once and read anywhere, with no ads and no tracking.

• Read offline: translations are downloaded once from eBible.org and stored on your device, so you never need a connection to read
• 1,500+ translations: the public domain World English Bible (WEB) is the default, with openly licensed translations in many languages
• Compare translations: view the same verse in several translations side by side
• Search every verse: full-text search, limited to the Old or New Testament, with quotation marks for exact matches, or jump straight to a reference like John 3:16
• Notes, bookmarks and highlights: saved as you type and shown inline in the text
• Listen: have a chapter read aloud with the voice and speed you choose, the current verse highlighted and media controls to pause or change chapter
• Share verses: copy or share with consecutive verses consolidated and verse numbers kept
• Reading progress: pick up exactly where you left off, and browse a timeline of what you have read by date or by book
• Quick navigation: tap a chapter to pick a verse, or open verses from bible:// links
• Comfortable reading: adjustable font size, light and dark themes, flowing paragraphs with headings and poetry, footnotes, and ruby text for Japanese and Chinese
• Your language: the interface is translated into several languages, including right-to-left scripts
• Tiny install: under 5 MB, no ads, no tracking, no analytics
• Bring your own Bibles: import USFM files or zips, add your own download sites, and check for updates to installed translations
• Private by design: your notes and progress stay on your device, and the app only goes online to download translations
• Open source: free software you can read, build and improve

The reader shows the 66 books of the Protestant canon. Many of the translation downloads on eBible.org also include the deuterocanon books.

Translations come from eBible.org as USFM files, including the public domain World English Bible (WEB). Most other translations are copyrighted with an open licence, so check each one's copyright and permissions.
